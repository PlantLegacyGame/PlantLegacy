# T56 — Volumetric Terrain

**Status:** `[~]` **partial — two slices, 2026-11-23.** Two authored regions are
real 3D volume meshed from signed-distance fields by surface nets: an arch near
spawn (an underside, an overhang and strata) and the soil under the Root Sight
aperture (a body with a shaft through it, so a buried eye sees earth instead of
the back of the soil sheet). The rest of the ground is still the T25 height
field; chunk streaming, per-voxel material ids, server collision, persistence
and LOD are **not built**. The row stays `[ ]`–equivalent (`[~]`) until the
acceptance criteria below are met, per the task rules.

ADR-043 scopes this: the first slice is a single authored region around spawn,
proving the field, the mesher and the materials on real gameplay before
planet-wide generation is attempted. T25's height field remains the surface truth
underneath.

## What changed

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| `packages/shared/src/world/surfaceNets.ts` | Pure surface-nets mesher. One vertex per sign-changing cell, one quad per sign-changing grid edge, gradient normals from the field. Welding is by construction: a cell owns its vertex, so neighbouring triangles cannot crack apart. |
| `packages/shared/src/world/terrainVolume.ts` | The region's signed-distance field: an elliptical rock band standing on its footing, cut to a flat width and cut off `leg_depth_m` below the *local* ground so the feet disappear into the soil. Bed fraction per vertex; region bounds with air on every side; deterministic wobble from the seed. |
| `packages/shared/src/world/terrainVolumeConfig.ts` | Reads `config/terrain_volume.json` through the same config loader the server uses. A missing key throws instead of building a different rock. |
| `apps/client/src/renderers/environment/terrainVolumeMesh.ts` | Turns the mesh arrays into a `BufferGeometry`, paints each bed from the soil palette's dry and wet earth (mixed 3:1 by `EnvironmentRenderer`), two shades plus grain deterministic in world position. |
| `packages/shared/src/world/apertureSoilVolume.ts` | Slice 2's field: a block of soil under the Root Sight aperture, its top 3 cm under the drawn surface, its floor `depth_m` below, and the aperture's void cut through it. Same mesher, same contract: bounds with air, a bed fraction per vertex, deterministic. |
| `apps/client/src/renderers/environment/apertureSoilMesh.ts` | Builds that body lazily for a buried eye, bands it in the dug wall's three soils, and fills it at the bowl's light level. |
| `EnvironmentRenderer` | Builds the region once at boot, after the ground, against `planetTerrainY` (the drawn surface, not the bare sphere); hides it with the surface shells when the eye is buried. |
| `clientConfig.ts` / `main.ts` | `/config/terrain_volume` is loaded with the other client configs and passed through `EnvironmentTuning.volume`. |

### The field

Negative inside the solid, positive in air. Three cuts intersected with `max`:

- **ring** — distance to the ellipse the band follows, `f / |∇f|`. Its centre is
  the footing, so the lower half is underground and the visible body is an arch.
  Half-thickness is wobbled by three seeded sinusoids and tapered to 72% at the
  crown, and the ring distance carries two octaves of deterministic value noise
  (6 cm and 1.2 cm), so the band is a rock with a weathered surface rather than
  a swept pipe.
- **width** — `|w| - width/2`, narrowed to 78% at the crown: a flat-sided bridge,
  not a tube.
- **footing** — `ground(x, z) - leg_depth - y`: closes each foot and keeps the
  buried half of the ellipse from poking out of a hillside. The 2.6 m depth is
  set from the crest the arch stands on: at 1.4 m the cut face still stood proud
  of the falling ground on its downhill side.

Footing is the **lowest of the four feet**, so a downhill foot reaches its ground
and an uphill one is buried. There is no plane the field could use instead.

### Measurement

The shipped region, flat ground, this machine: **2 386 vertices, 4 780
triangles, 52 ms** (Node 24). It is built once at boot, not per frame. Vertical
extent −2.6 m (the foot cut) to +3.18 m (crown plus half-thickness); **725
vertices face downwards**, which is the underside the height field could not
have.

### The visual pass (2026-11-23)

Four screenshots of the first build - through the opening, end-on, from the
plant and with the eye buried - found five things, all fixed and re-shot:

| Found | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A smooth teal slab, then a concrete one | Vertex colour was `soil_mineral_tint` (a blue accent the soil shader uses for mineral grains), then a 15% mix of it | Dry soil pulled a quarter of the way to wet, and only 6% mineral: rock is earth, not the accent |
| Extruded pipe silhouette, flat road-like top | Constant half-thickness, no height taper | Thickness tapers to 72% and width to 78% at the crown; a third wobble term runs in height as well as angle |
| Clay-smooth surface, no grain | One low-frequency field only | Two octaves of value noise on the ring distance (6 cm, 1.2 cm) plus a 0.8..1.2 bed ramp in the vertex colours |
| Uphill leg buried, downhill leg tangent to the skin (speckled z-fighting) | The first site had **2.83 m** of relief across the arch's footprint | Moved to (21, −11): **1.28 m** of relief, base 1.0 m above the plant's ground and 0.09 m clear of every crest between it and spawn, opening bearing 63° at the plant |
| A hard cut face standing proud of the falling ground | 1.4 m of leg below a crest whose ground falls 1.8 m in 3.5 m | `leg_depth_m` 2.6: the rock now reaches 2.55 m under the soil at its feet (measured in the live scene) |

One attempted fix failed and was removed: a small noise on the *width* cut (to
weather the flat sides seen end-on) opened one non-manifold edge in the mesh.
`terrainVolume.test.ts`'s watertight assertion caught it, and the field kept the
flat width cut rather than trade a closed surface for a shape detail. End-on the
arch is still a tapered plank; that is the remaining soft spot, and it is a
silhouette, not a hole.

The same pass answered the ordering question: the arch proves the pipeline in
front of the player, but the buried-eye screenshot is the back of the soil's
single sheet. The ground under the Root Sight aperture was the next slice.

### Slice 2: the ground under the aperture (2026-11-23)

A buried eye at the aperture saw the back of `planet-terrain-detail`: one opaque
sheet, no thickness, with the opening floating in it
(`screenshots/aperture-buried-before.png`). The sheet is still the surface above.
What changed is that the soil under the aperture is now a body.

- **Field** (`apertureSoilVolume.ts`): solid where the point is under the drawn
  surface, above a floor `depth_m` down, inside a plan extent, and outside the
  aperture's void; meshed by surface nets. The top follows `ground(x, z)`, so on
  the spawn hillside - 5.0 m of relief across the region - it is a wedge of
  earth, not a lid.
- **Fit**: the top sits 3 cm *under* the sheet. From above, the opaque sheet is
  in front and nothing changes; from below, the body is in front of the sheet's
  back. No shader cut, no coincident surface. The shaft is the bowl's mouth plus
  2 cm, so the tuned bowl still lines the hole and the body never shows through
  it.
- **Build**: lazily, only when the eye is buried inside the region, keyed to the
  aperture's centre, mouth and depth. 3 718 vertices / 7 448 triangles at the
  seedling's live aperture; hidden above ground.
- **Cost, and the column cache**: the mesher samples the field once per grid
  point, and the gradient normals add six samples per vertex, so asking
  `planetTerrainY` for its height at each sample evaluated the same (x, z)
  column dozens of times - ~50 000 terrain calls for ~1 200 columns, and that was
  the whole build. `buildApertureSoilMesh` now samples the drawn surface once per
  column and interpolates between columns; the field is only ever read at grid
  points, where the interpolation is exact.
- **Measurement (browser, 2026-11-23)**: the first buried frame - a seedling's
  0.225 m aperture, built by the render loop itself - spent **95.3 ms** in the
  build before the column cache and takes **22.1 / 23.6 ms** after it (two runs,
  same aperture and ground), with the frame at 99.9 ms before and **33.1 ms**
  after against a 16.7 ms median. With the sag slab and the rim snap the same
  first build measured **42.4 / 49.9 / 68.7 ms** across page loads and
  **17.5 / 19.2 / 24.8 ms** for repeat builds in the same page, frame median
  16.7 ms. The slab's own share was then isolated with a temporary skip flag in
  the same page, so both arms ran the same code: 14 builds each way at the
  seedling's aperture read 13.6 vs 12.3 ms in one order and 17.1 vs 13.6 in the
  other, and 20 interleaved builds each way at a grown 0.82 m aperture read
  **12.5 ms with the slab against 11.0 ms without** (means 13.1 and 11.8) -
  **about 1.5 ms, a tenth of the build**, where the slab is 1 521 field samples
  and its widening shows in the body (3 830 vertices against 3 797). What the
  slab does not explain is the cold spread: first builds in fresh pages have read
  40.1 and 48.6 ms with the slab, 27.8, 29.7, 30.0 and 40.2 without it, and
  251.5 ms once for a grown aperture with it, while warm repeats of the same
  grown build settle at 12-13 ms like the seedling's. Nothing measured here
  accounts for a first build moving between 22 and 251 ms, so the cause of that
  spread is left as unexplained rather than attributed; what a rebuild costs is
  the 12-25 ms warm figure. Rebuilds while the plant grew into a 0.82 m
  aperture measured 28.7, 12.6 and 10.4 ms at 0.63, 0.78 and 0.82 m. Direct
  builds at 1.4 m and 4.9 m before the cache were 84 ms (7 352 triangles) and
  409 ms (31 648): the cost is the terrain sample, not the mesher, which is why
  the earlier 131 ms Node figure (flat ground) understated the hillside.
- **Light**: one topsoil fill at 0.34 of the scene's cavity light level. A
  per-vertex fill was tried for the bands - a white emissive times `vColor` - and
  cut, measured: at the seedling's aperture the fill costs nothing worth having,
  because the three bands sit too close in luminance for the emissive term to
  carry them. It was also broken in a way worth recording: this three declares
  `varying vec4 vColor`, so `emissive * vColor` does not compile and the body
  drew nothing at all (the renderer's log carried `program not valid` and the
  aperture showed the bowl's shell alone). The bands ride the lit surfaces'
  vertex colours, as on the pit's wall; `apertureSoilMesh.test.ts` pins the tint,
  the light scaling and the absence of a shader patch.
- **Fit, and what the fit test caught**: the builder used to trust the mesher to
  leave the shaft where the field put it, but surface nets lands vertices near
  the zero crossing, not on it. At a seedling's 0.29 m mouth (cells are 0.26 m)
  82 vertices sat up to **27 mm inside the bowl's mouth** - soil in front of the
  wall the bowl lines. Vertices nearer the axis than the void are pushed back
  out to its radius, and the test then found the faces: vertices on the void are
  joined by chords that dip inside it, by up to 14 mm at that mouth. The field
  now widens the wall *below the top* by the sag it measures from the mesh
  itself, and the top keeps the 2 cm cut that meets the sheet's edge. Checked
  live with the cavity probe's tint - bowl green, soil red: from above, every
  pixel of a grown opening is bowl, none is soil.
- **The shaft's widening, measured, not guessed**: the sag depends on the
  polygon surface nets happens to produce, so the builder meshes a small snapped
  slab of the shaft alone (same step and grid alignment, top two centimetres
  below the sheet) and takes its innermost face; the wall is the void plus that
  sag plus 4 mm. At a seedling's 0.225 m aperture (cells are 0.26 m) the wall
  lands at **0.276 m** and its faces at **0.247 m**, outside the 0.229 m mouth
  and flush with the void. An earlier guess of three times a nominal chord
  (`3 step^2 / 8 void`) stood at 0.35 m and was as wide as the bowl; `f = 2.0 -
  2.7` of the void's own radius is what the probe says the mesh needs.
- **The ring around the bowl, and what it was**: from a buried eye the earth's
  opening was drawn at radius **0.384 m** around a 0.229 m mouth - 15.5 cm of sky
  around the bowl, a barrel. Wall widening could not touch it: the top's annulus
  is cut at the void, but surface nets places those vertices at cell centres, so
  the drawn hole is the mesh's own polygon, most of a cell out. The builder now
  snaps the rim's vertices onto the void's circle at the top plane, reading the
  surface height again after the move (the ground can fall across it). The
  opening measures **0.249 m**, a 2.0 cm ring - the gap the body was always cut
  with - and `apertureSoilMesh.test.ts` pins it. The wall below keeps the sag's
  margin, so the stored circle overhangs the widened wall by 2.7 cm: an earth
  lip, not a barrel.

Seen from under the aperture (`screenshots/aperture-buried-side.png`) the eye now
has strata bands, a floor and a lit shaft. The surface view of the pit
(`screenshots/aperture-surface-after.png`) is unchanged: the same bowl, rim and
seed in the same hole, and `aperture-soil-volume.visible` is false above ground.

Looking down into a grown pit (0.82 m, seven nodes) after the fit tests: with the
cavity probe's tint on - bowl green, soil red - every pixel of the opening is
bowl, so the shaft fixes put no earth inside it. The wall reads as topsoil,
subsoil and the grey deep floor. Two things read wrong and were fixed on the
way: the floor ring carried the wall's own radial clod relief, so the cap's edge
was a scalloped ring rather than a circle (`strataShell.test.ts`), and
underground the body's fill was one tinted colour, so its bands disappeared - a
per-vertex fill was tried for them and cut on measurement (above). What still looks ragged is the deep band's contour, which follows the
clodded wall by design - a shading edge, not a hole.

## 4. Why this shape, and what was cut

- **Not a replacement for the cavity bowl.** The bowl is behind a *dynamic*
  aperture and carries about twenty fixed defects in its rim conform, near-half
  cut, buried-eye sightline and water clip. A static surface-nets mesh cannot be
  cut per frame without re-meshing, and replacing the tuned shell blind would
  risk all of them. The volume goes where it is additive and verifiable first.
- **Not planet-wide.** ADR-043 says one region. A planet-wide grid at 0.3 m is
  tens of billions of samples.
- **No spurious API.** `isSolidAt`, `columnSpans`, chunk ids, a revision counter
  and a binary snapshot were drafted and dropped: nothing in the game reads them
  yet, and this repo treats a tested-only function as dead code. They arrive with
  the slice that needs them.
- **No second feature.** A stacked shelf would have doubled the field code to
  prove a case the arch already proves (a column with rock above air above
  ground). Caves are the next authored shape.
