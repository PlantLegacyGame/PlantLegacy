# T04 — Mutation

**Status:** `[~]` **partial.** (Full locomotion open; everything else verified in a
running client on 2026-11-14, see "Browser verification".) Data-driven mutations, adaptive pressure, maintenance
with a global scale, shedding, expanded effect families, twelve visual signatures
and protocol v2 are implemented. `movement.reroot_speed` is now consumed by the T06
reroot crawl (2026-11-15). The acceptance criterion's second half,
translocation, is **not met**: it is post-MVP locomotion and no task owns it yet.
T04 stays partial until that locomotion block is scheduled.
**Phase:** 1 — The Living Sprout. **Depends on:** T00A, T01, T03.
**Config owner:** `config/mutations.json` (`active`), plus `config/net_proto.json`
(protocol version) and `config/plant_renderer.json` (signature strengths).
**Decisions:** ADR-050 (gameplay before measurement), ADR-051 (roots take
resources, not HP), ADR-053 (chance plus legible pressure), ADR-054 (twelve
signatures as an index).

## Catalogue (27 definitions)

Broad families: cosmetic (colour/tissue), stat (diet, energy metabolism, growth
speed), structural (plant and root size), ability (new growth nodes, movement,
nocturnal habit). How a mutation looks is **not** in this file: every definition
has one style record in `config/mutation_visuals.json`, keyed by its id
(ADR-059), so a name can never share another mutation's look. The style owns the
organ family it paints, its pattern, tint, variegation, glow, bark
tickness/darkening and thorn density; the ordinal of that record in declaration
order is what ships as `visualSignature` on the wire.

| family | ids | what it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| light / energy | `bioluminescent_lamina`, `photic_nodes`, `ravenous_fixation`, `symbiotic_nodules`, `nocturnal_habit`, `sparse_metabolism`, `fatty_reserve` | `photosynthesis_multiplier` (energy per second of light), `energy_upkeep_multiplier` |
| pigment / tissue | `variegated_foliage`, `rubescent_pigment`, `glaucous_wax`, `pale_tissue`, `gold_mottle`, `violet_lamina`, `ashen_cuticle`, `copper_pigment` | tint, variegation and glow signatures |
| bark / structure | `thickened_bark`, `umbral_bark`, `thick_cortex`, `deep_taproot`, `columnar_habit`, `drooping_fronds`, `spined_epidermis` | `size_multiplier`, `root_size_multiplier`, bark scale |

| other play | `motile_crown` | `reroot_speed_multiplier` + `movement.reroot_speed` (consumed by T06) |

## The basic plant (what a new seed is)

A new plant is a single seed node: **1 biomass**, a dark brown ovoid capsule
(`0x8a6d4d`), resting on the soil surface, with **water and minerals at
capacity** (capacity comes from its own root biomass, not a literal) and energy
at the configured starting fraction. Nothing else exists yet.

The first four growths define the basic plant, in this order:

| stage | biomass | segment | time | look | notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| radicle | 8 | 0.85 m | 8 s | warm brown root (`0x684632`) | grows downward, first root, can roll a mutation |
| cotyledon | 6 | 0.1 m | 6 s | yellow-green seed leaf (`0x7fa24d`) | max 2; photosynthesis 1.2, light blocking 0.8 |
| stem | 12 | 0.35 m | 10 s | green-brown shoot (`0x526b38`) | only after the crown is established |
| leaf | 6 | 0.08 m | 9 s | green blade (`0x4a7c59`) | photosynthesis 1.5 |

So the "basic plant" is: one seed capsule, one taproot, two seed leaves, then a
short shoot with leaves. It is deliberately small, entirely non-mutated, and
fully stocked with water/minerals so the first decisions are about light and
growth, not about finding water.

## Starter mutations (every player's first rolls)

Six definitions carry `starter: true` in config. **While a plant holds no
mutation, the acquisition roll only considers starters** (service.ts), so the
first mutation any player gets is one of these simple, low-upkeep looks/stats:

| id | look | gate | base upkeep/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `variegated_foliage` | mottled leaves (sig 3) | none | 0.045 |
| `glaucous_wax` | blue-grey waxy bloom (sig 5) | none | 0.068 |
| `rubescent_pigment` | red-tinged tissue (sig 4) | light >= 55 | 0.068 |
| `ashen_cuticle` | pale ash cuticle (sig 6) | light >= 20 | 0.045 |
| `copper_pigment` | copper tint (sig 4) | light >= 40 | 0.060 |
| `sparse_metabolism` | thinner tissue (sig 8) | light <= 60 | 0.045 |

Upkeep shown is the base bill before the node/biomass scaling from the previous
section; a full-grown plant pays more.

Two of them have no environment gate at all, so **every player can always roll
at least one starter**; the others are still reachable in ordinary daylight.
Everything else — vines, petals, movement, sizes, storage — unlocks after the
first mutation, once the plant has demonstrated it can carry one.

## Scope delivered

### Config (`config/mutations.json`)

- 36 mutation definitions as `mutation_*` object entries across the requested
  families: visuals/colour, diet and energy metabolism, plant/root size, growth
  speed, new growth nodes (`unlock_node_types`) and new ways to play
  (`reroot_speed_multiplier`). Adding a mutation is a config edit.
- `maintenance_scale` (1.5) multiplies every mutation's energy upkeep, so a
  mutation always costs more than not holding it.
- Each definition carries `kind`, `gene_tags`, `triggers` (chance weight,
  light/moisture/minerals gates, weather list, day/night), `maintenance`,
  `effects`, `movement` and `shed`. Its appearance comes from
  `config/mutation_visuals.json` (ADR-059): `target` is the organ family it
  paints (`foliage` / `woody` / `seed` / `bloom` / `whole`), so a mutation only
  changes what it names. `visualSignature`, `visualParams` and `visualTarget`
  are derived from the style at load, so the wire shape did not change.
- Shape mutations are behaviour as well as paint: `columnar_habit` carries
  `vertical_bias` and `drooping_fronds` `drooping_bias`, applied by
  `applyMutationShapeBias` before the gene clamp, and `spined_epidermis` adds
  `spines` geometry to woody tubes.
- Appearance is composed, not selected (ADR-059): every held mutation
  contributes to the organ families it targets, so a plant shows its bark
  mutation on the trunk and its lamina mutation on the leaves at the same time.
  Tints multiply and clamp, variegation and darkening stack as `1 - prod(1 - v)`,
  glow sums, and `caps` bounds bark thickness, darkening and thorns.
- No `rarity` field or rarity language anywhere (RULE_20).
- `node_type_to_category` and `category_to_tag_map` are config maps, not code.

### Server

- `ops/mutations/types.ts` — vocabulary, eight categories, definition shape.
- `ops/mutations/config.ts` — loader/validator; throws at startup on a duplicate
  id, a missing `id`, or a signature outside 1..12.
- `ops/mutations/pressure.ts` — exponential decay, investment accumulation and a
  saturating pressure multiplier capped by `max_pressure_multiplier`.
- `ops/mutations/service.ts` — acquisition roll, maintenance, effect products,
  dominant signature, shedding.
- `PlantWorld`:
  - `setMutations`, `setMutationEnvironmentProvider`, `setMutationRandom`.
  - `maybeAcquireMutation` runs on every successful growth that adds a node.
  - `recordInvestment` feeds pressure from the grown node type and its biomass.
  - `chargeMutationMaintenance` bills every held mutation in the active upkeep
    path **and** the dormant catch-up path; the bill is paid out of energy, then
    biomass, and biomass stops at `upkeep_biomass_floor`. It never sheds a
    mutation on its own and never kills - an unpaid bill stalls the plant.
  - `growthCostMultiplier` applies to the quoted energy cost.
  - `shedMutation` refuses during cooldown, removes the mutation and applies the
    biomass penalty.
  - `applyMutationSignature` stamps every node so all payload builders carry it.
- Events: `mutation_acquired`, `mutation_shed`, `mutation_leveled` on the event bus.
- Size mutations are tissue (ADR-057): `syncMutationMass` fills each part to
  `quota × scale³` when the set grows, and `absorbOversizeMass` drains the
  excess gradually when it shrinks; radii always come from biomass, never a
  shader scale.
- Levels (ADR-058): `level_up` in config gives the per-level cost, card
  steps and card labels. `offerMutationLevel` deals the mutation's own cards,
  `chooseMutationLevel` spends the resources, applies the card and grows the
  body into the new form. Levels and cards persist in PLNT v4.
- Persistence: PLNT snapshot `v2` writes mutations, the eight pressure values,
  the shed cooldown and the signature. The reader still accepts `v1`.
- Protocol v2: `MUTATION_STATE` (35), `MUTATION_SHED` (36).

### Client

- `renderers/plant/mutations.ts` — twelve signature names, name → index, index
  reads from either server shape, and a per-signature style table.
- `PlantMeshBuilder` packs the signature index in bits 28–31 and scales drawn
  radii for bark signatures.
- `mesh.wgsl` / `billboard.wgsl` read the four-bit index and apply glow, bark
  darkening, mottling and pigment tints across all twelve.
- `PlantRenderer.updatePlantData` maps the plant's strongest signature onto the
  SDF path's three mutation uniforms (lower fidelity, documented in ADR-054).
- `ui/MutationPanel.ts` — mutation list with per-mutation upkeep, a shed control
  that sends `MUTATION_SHED`, and the eight-category pressure readout. Plain
  `textContent`, no innerHTML (INV-NET-15), no strategy copy (RULE_13).

### Upkeep scales with what a mutation holds on to

A mutation's energy bill is `base × maintenance_scale × (1 + node_scale ×
affected_nodes + biomass_scale × affected_biomass)`, capped by
`upkeep_max_multiplier`. "Affected" is kind-aware: root-shaped mutations (those
with a root size effect) bill the root system, everything else bills the canopy.
Tunables: `mutation_upkeep_node_scale` 0.05, `mutation_upkeep_biomass_scale` 0.01,
`mutation_upkeep_max_multiplier` 3. Covered by a unit test that builds canopy and
root nodes and checks both the formula and the root/canopy split.

## Growth tissues and mutation families (T04 extension)

New node types, all config-driven (`config/growth.json`) and mutation-gated:

| node type | layer | role | unlocked by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `trunk` | canopy | secondary woody stem; the only tissue bark thickens | `thickened_bark`, `woody_trunk`, `hollow_trunk` |
| `rhizome` | root | underground horizontal stem; can surface into a new shoot | `clonal_rhizome`, `clonal_network` |
| `culm` | canopy | hollow segmented shoot: cheap, fast, holds water | `hollow_culm`, `segmented_culm` |
| `floating_leaf` | canopy | buoyant pad that lies on the water surface | `floating_pads` |
| `pneumatophore` | root | aerial breathing root lifted out of waterlogged soil | `aerenchyma` |
| `thorn` | canopy | short, rigid, mineral-dense defensive shoot | `spined_epidermis` |
| `stolon` | canopy | above-ground runner with long internodes; roots at a node and raises a shoot | `clonal_network` |
| `tuber` | root | swollen starch/water storage stem at the end of a rhizome; eyes are buds | `clonal_rhizome` |

Bark is secondary growth only: `lookAppliesTo` maps the `bark` target to `trunk`/`branch`, and the
WebGPU radius pass thickens only those nodes, so a young stem is never barked or repainted.

New mutation families (all effects are simulated today):

- **Hollow stems / water storage** — `hollow_culm` (unlock culm, water ×1.5, growth speed ×1.3),
  `segmented_culm` (unlock culm, growth speed ×1.15), `hollow_trunk` (unlock trunk, water ×1.4),
  `woody_trunk` (unlock trunk, root size ×1.1).
- **Clonal root systems** — `clonal_rhizome` (unlock rhizome + tuber, water ×1.15,
  `stored_resource_sharing` 0.25), `clonal_network` (unlock rhizome + stolon, mineral ×1.2,
  upkeep ×0.95, sharing 0.4). A rhizome is a root-layer parent that can cross into the canopy, which
  is the game's route to a second shoot from the same organism.
- **Aquatic habit** — `floating_pads` (unlock floating_leaf, photosynthesis ×1.25, water ×1.1),
  `aerenchyma` (unlock pneumatophore, water ×1.3, photosynthesis ×1.05).
- **Defense** — `spined_epidermis` (unlock thorn, upkeep ×1.04). A thorn adds `defense` and a high
  `structural_integrity` to the part carrying it.
- **Material types** — `silica_cuticle` (upkeep ×0.95, size ×1.02), plus the existing bark and
  pigment set.

Stolons and tubers are modelled from the standard botany descriptions rather than invented:

- **Stolon (runner)** — above-ground horizontal shoots with long internodes that root adventitiously
  at a node and can raise a new plant there; strawberries are the classic example
  ([LibreTexts, *Stem Modifications*](https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Botany/Botany_%28Ha_Morrow_and_Algiers%29/03%253A_Plant_Structure/3.03%253A_Stems/3.3.04%253A_Stem_Modifications);
  [U. Minnesota, *Unique Storage Organs*](https://open.lib.umn.edu/horticulture/chapter/10-2-unique-storage-organs)).
  In game terms it is the one canopy organ that may place a `root`, and its node may also raise a
  `stem`.
- **Tuber** — a thick, short, below-ground storage stem formed at the tip of a rhizome or stolon, its
  "eyes" being lateral buds; starch storage is its function
  ([LibreTexts, *Stem Modifications*](https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Botany/Botany_%28Ha_Morrow_and_Algiers%29/03%253A_Plant_Structure/3.03%253A_Stems/3.3.04%253A_Stem_Modifications)).
  It carries `water_storage`/`starch_storage`, a short thick radius profile, and can raise a shoot
  from its own buds.

Botanical basis: bamboo culms are hollow, lack bark, and reach full height in one season on water
and carbohydrates moved through connected rhizomes; water lilies float on broad pads with
aerenchyma (air tissue) and thick cuticles; clonal stands such as aspen share one root system and
identical DNA. The simulation expresses these through the effects it already owns (unlocked node
types, storage capacity, growth speed, upkeep) rather than promising unimplemented mechanics.

### Clonal sharing

A clonal mutation now carries `stored_resource_sharing` (0..0.9): the share of the stored
water/mineral gate the network covers for a part grown **from root tissue**. `canGrow` takes the
sharing factor, so the same number is used by the server's growth check and by the growth menu's
availability quote (`buildGrowthOptions`); a shoot grown from a rhizome therefore no longer has to
hold the full reserve on its own. Energy remains the individual plant's, which is the honest model:
the network moves water and minerals, not fixed carbon. Sharing is a capability, not a product —
the widest network the plant holds wins.

### Aquatic gate

A `floating_leaf` intent is refused with `floating_leaf_needs_water` unless the tile under the point
it would occupy has at least `floating_leaf_min_groundwater` (config, default 20). The mutation
unlocks the organ; the water table decides whether it can float there.

### Tissue health

Health is **derived, not stored**, in `packages/shared/growth/health.ts`:

- `tissueMassQuota` is the mass a finished part of that type carries: `biomass_cost ×
  growth_biomass_yield`. The yield (0.6 in `config/growth.json`) is the share of the carbon budget
  that stays as tissue; measuring against the raw cost would mark every finished, untouched plant as
  40% damaged.
- `nodeHealth` is `biomass / quota`, and a part whose `growthProgress < 1` reads 1: a shoot that is
  still filling in has not had the chance to lose mass. Before this rule a fresh cotyledon with no
  biomass yet read zero health for a tick or two, which could refuse the next growth.
- `plantHealth` is the mass-weighted mean over every part, seed excluded.

Damage is real: when upkeep cannot pay for water or minerals, `shedTissueMass` takes the shortfall
out of the parts in proportion to the mass each holds (so `totalBiomass` stays the sum of its
nodes). A fed plant then regrows missing tissue at `tissue_repair_per_second`, paying
`tissue_repair_energy_per_biomass`, so health recovers instead of ratcheting down. Growth completion
never refills mass a drought took.

Two consumers:

- photosynthesis: `health_photosynthesis_floor` (0.5) is the share kept at zero health, so a wilting
  plant fixes less carbon — applied in the one place energy is credited, which both the online loop
  and the offline catch-up use;
- growth: `canGrow` refuses with a health reason below `growth_min_health` (0.35), so a plant must
  rebuild tissue before it extends.

No new wire field or save column was needed, which is why health cannot drift out of sync with the
plant. The client's older per-node `health` estimate (`biomass / 10`) is still what the renderer
sees; the authoritative figure is server-side.

### Why one stem growth was refused (2026-11-14, later run)

A stem intent in a scripted browser run came back `insufficient_stored_resources`. The server
logged no rule rejection, and the growth menu quoted the numbers: `storedWater 0` against a need of
12, moments after `dev_fill_resources` had set it to 46. The culprit was the verification script
itself: it called `dev_fill_biomass`, which sets `totalBiomass` to `max_nodes_per_plant ×
node_cost_base × 10` (200 000). Water and mineral upkeep scale with total biomass, so a 200 k-mass
plant burns ~46 stored water in well under a second and the gate then refuses the intent it was
meant to allow. The same intent grows normally with the fills alone.

Covered by `drains the stores when biomass is inflated past what the plant holds` in
`apps/server/src/ops/plantWorld.test.ts`, which reproduces the collapse and the gate reason, and by
`grows a stem immediately after the second cotyledon`. Scripted growth chains should fill energy and
stored resources, not biomass.

### Mutation gallery (2026-11-15)

`dev_mutation_gallery` (Dev Panel → Plant → Mutation Gallery) builds one plant per mutation on an
eight-column grid, ten metres apart, placed in front of the player who asked for it. A showcase
plant is granted its mutation **first** — so the organs it unlocks are legal growth targets and its
size shapes the tissue that grows into it — and then grown to a mature body: radicle and three
lateral roots, two cotyledons, a stem, a five-internode column (a `trunk`/`culm` column when the
mutation unlocked one), leaves alternating up the column, branches off each internode with their own
leaves, thorns when `spined_epidermis` is held, a rhizome with tuber and stolon, a pneumatophore, and
blooms on branch tips for a bloom mutation. It is then filled to its own mutated quota
(`fillPlantTissue`), which is what makes a size mutation visible at full size. Typical bodies are
20–34 nodes.

Growth intents are still sent through `tryGrow`, so the gene sectors apply (a columnar habit still
bends the column). Anything the rules refuse is returned in `rejected` as
`<mutation>:<organ>:<reason>` instead of silently thinning the row. Rival plants carry a sprite name
tag above the canopy: the mutation name for gallery plants, mutation plus owner tag for players.
Rebuilding replaces the previous grid. Covered by `mutation gallery` in
`apps/server/src/ops/plantWorld.test.ts`, which asserts a mature body (≥15 nodes, full growth),
every required organ, and that the same body plan weighs more with a size mutation.

The gallery is also the fastest way to eyeball a new node type's art against every other mutation,
and the tags make "which one is this?" a glance instead of a guess.
