# AL-04 — Atmosphere, sky and post

**Status:** in progress, started 2026-11-15 (P1). Shared atmosphere model landed
and the ground and plant materials now use it; sky, water and post remain.

## Why this comes first

`docs/abyssal-mechanisms.md` lesson 4: the perceived jump from 1.0 to 1.x came
from air, not geometry. Before P1 the same distance was described four times,
independently:

- `THREE.Fog` on the scene (`EnvironmentRenderer`),
- a fog tint inside the ground shader (`environment/ground.ts`),
- another inside the plant SDF shader (`renderers/plant/sdfShaders.ts`),
- the sky dome's own two-colour gradient, plus water's separate absorption.

So a hill, the grass on it and the plant standing on it faded differently, and
no single config key could fix the mismatch.

## What landed

`apps/client/src/renderers/environment/atmosphere.ts` — one model, two mirrored
forms:

- **TypeScript:** `heightDensity`, `airMass` (Kasten–Young), `transmittance`,
  `phaseHG` (normalised so an isotropic lobe is 1), `inScatterColor`,
  `applyAtmosphere`, `atmosphereUniformValues`.
- **GLSL:** `GLSL_ATMOSPHERE`, a snippet with the same maths and the same
  constants (`atmoHeightDensity`, `atmoAirMass`, `atmoTransmittance`,
  `atmoPhaseHG`, `atmoInScatter`, `atmoApply`) that materials include instead of
  writing their own fog.

Config (`config/render.json`, flat `/config/render` keys, no new config file):
`atmosphere_extinction_r/g/b`, `atmosphere_scale_height_m`,
`atmosphere_sun_phase_g`, `atmosphere_in_scatter`, `atmosphere_horizon_boost`,
normalised into `RenderConfig` with clamps and fallbacks.

The defaults are art-directed for this world (visibility measured in hundreds of
metres, hills hundreds of metres high), not Earth air: colour is extinguished
per channel so distance cools, density falls with height so ridges stand out of
the haze, and a horizon boost makes grazing views heavier than the zenith.

## Wired so far

- **Ground** (`environment/ground.ts`): the standard material's own scene fog is
  off (`fog: false`); the shader gets `GLSL_ATMOSPHERE` and the five uniforms, and
  applies the model in linear space just before the colour-space conversion.
  Height above the surface is radial distance from the planet centre, not world
  y, so haze behaves on a 100 km sphere. `setGroundAtmosphere` caches the params
  on the material and also updates the compiled shader, so a call before the
  first compile cannot leave stale defaults.
- **Plant SDF** (`plant/sdfShaders.ts`, `PlantRenderer`): the shader's own
  `exp(-0.002 * dist²)` fog is gone. It applies the same `atmoApply` on hit
  pixels, in linear space before its gamma conversion, so a plant at distance
  fades into the same air as the terrain behind it. `PlantRenderer.setAtmosphere`
  is fed the environment's model each frame.
- One model instance: `EnvironmentRenderer.getAtmosphere()`.

- **Mesh plants** (`environment/standardAtmosphere.ts`, `MeshPlantRenderer`):
  every organ material is patched in place - scene fog off, a world-space
  varying added, the model applied in linear space before the colour-space
  conversion. Cloned mutation variants are patched as they are created, and the
  sun direction is shared by reference with the SDF uniforms. This closed the
  last seam between near plants, far plants and the ground.

Water and the sky are the remaining `scene.fog` consumers.

## Next in P1

1. Move water and the remaining `scene.fog` consumers (mesh plants, rain, the
   cavity wall) onto the same uniforms, then drop the scene fog entirely.
2. Analytic sky scattering replaces the gradient dome; clouds from scrolling fBm
   driven by weather coverage; stars at night.
3. Half-res light shafts, then desktop-only post (bloom, tonemap, dither) as the
   band controller's third lever.

Known constraint: shafts cannot be occluded by plants while the plant pass lives
on the separate WebGPU canvas. That waits for AL-02's unified frame.

## Verification

Unit tests cover the model's shape: density and transmittance bounds, blue
extinguishing fastest, air mass overhead versus horizon, forward-peaked phase,
zero distance leaving a surface unchanged, a distant surface converging on the
in-scatter colour, config clamping, and that every GLSL uniform has a value and
the two mirrors share one phase normalisation.

The visual acceptance is the six AL-01 poses, judged by eye; no test asserts
that the air looks right. In the browser the ground and plant shaders both
compiled (no shader errors in the console), the scene kept drawing, and the
pixel ratio stayed at native through load, but the fog gradient itself has not
been compared side by side with a screenshot.
